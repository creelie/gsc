\section{Discussion and open problems}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What has been shown}

A passive system can delay a band for free, but it cannot shift the phase of
the whole band by a constant without loss. The least mean attenuation is
$\theta\Lambda$, with $\Lambda=(K(\kappa)-E(\kappa))/\kappa$, and it is attained.
The proof is one sum rule, \eqref{eq:sumrule}, built from the test function
$W$; the lead bound, the worst-frequency and geometric-mean bounds, the bound
for anomalous dispersion and the stability under perturbations of the phase
follow from it or from the extremal system.

For an affine phase every zero-free system is the extremal function plus
terms that each raise the attenuation at every point of the band
(\cref{thm:reconstruction}), so one system is optimal at every frequency at
once. With zeros the sum rule is lost, but after the change of variables of
\cref{lem:transform} a comparison argument gives the same kind of pointwise
optimum, attained by a system with one zero, whose profile is elliptic
(\cref{thm:ellipticprofile}). At a right angle the system is algebraic and
$\abs{H(\sqrt{ab})}^2\le a/b$.

With a delay the picture changes. Without zeros the least mean loss is
known exactly for every slope and stays above a positive floor. With zeros
the optimum exists, is unique and is characterised by the inequality of
\cref{thm:convex}(ii); it is the limit of finite convex problems over
Toeplitz contractions (\cref{thm:construction}), has no zeros inside the band
(\cref{thm:regularity}), and its loss decays exponentially with the delay,
at a rate at least $\frac{a+b}\pi\log\cot\frac{\pi(b-a)}{4(a+b)}$
(\cref{thm:sharperate}).

\subsection{Open problems}

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] \emph{The optimum for a delay with zeros.} For
$\arg H\equiv\theta+\tau\omega$ with $\tau>0$, find $H_{\theta+\tau\omega}$ of
\cref{thm:convex} in closed form, or at least the exact exponent in
\cref{cor:rate}; we only know that it lies between $-\pi ab/(b-a)$ and
$-C_{a,b}$ of \cref{cor:rate}. \Cref{thm:construction} computes the optimum at each fixed delay
but gives no rate of convergence, and it says nothing about the limit
$\tau\to\infty$. The change of variables of \cref{lem:transform} needs a
constant phase, and whether $H_{\theta+\tau\omega}$ is continuous at $a$ and $b$
is not known.
\item[(2)] \emph{General phase without zeros.} \Cref{cor:compare} is sharp
whenever $h_g+\max\{0,-\beta_g\}R/z$ is a Herglotz function. For an affine
phase \cref{thm:exactmean} finds the cheapest measure off the band in every
case; for a phase that is not affine and for which $v_g$ changes sign it is
not known.
\item[(3)] \emph{Several channels.} \Cref{thm:trace} is sharp for
$\Theta\succeq0$, and \cref{prop:commuting} reduces commuting $\Theta,T$ to
scalars. For indefinite $\Theta$ not commuting with $T$ the least trace is not
known, and by
\cref{thm:noloewner} there is in general no least attenuation in the
Loewner order.
\item[(4)] \emph{Several bands.} \Cref{thm:multiband} decides when a given
compensation works, and with zeros allowed \cref{cor:allbands} gives the
optimum as a limit. The sharp constant without zeros, a period of a
hyperelliptic differential in place of $\Lambda$, has not been computed.
\item[(5)] \emph{Reflection and matching.} The Fano bound \cite{fano} and the
present bound constrain different parts of a reflection coefficient. A
joint bound on its modulus and argument over a band would combine them.
\item[(6)] \emph{Formalisation.} The finite arithmetic is checked in Lean
(\cref{app:verification}). The analytic part, which needs boundary values of
Cauchy integrals, Hardy spaces and the complete elliptic integrals, is not
machine-checked.
\end{enumerate}
