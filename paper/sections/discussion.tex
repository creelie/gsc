\section{Discussion and open problems}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What has been shown}

A passive system can delay a band of frequencies for free, but it cannot
shift the phase of the whole band by a constant without losing transmitted
power. The least mean attenuation is $\theta\Lambda$, with $\Lambda=(K(\kappa)-E(\kappa))/\kappa$
an explicit function of the band ratio, and the bound is attained. The proof
is a single sum rule, \eqref{eq:sumrule}, built from the test function $W$,
and every other statement of the paper is a consequence of it or of the
explicit extremal system: the lead bound, the worst-frequency and
geometric-mean bounds, the bound for anomalous dispersion in a medium, and
the stability under perturbations of the phase.

For an affine phase there is more: every zero-free system is the extremal
function $h_g$ plus terms that each raise the attenuation at every point of
the band (\cref{thm:reconstruction}), so the extremal system, or its
inversion for a lead, has the least attenuation at every frequency of the
band at once. This gives the sharp worst frequency and the sharp plain mean
for a lead (\cref{cor:pointwisecons}).

With zeros allowed the sum rule is no longer available, and the problem
changes character. After the change of variables of \cref{lem:transform}
the zeros disappear, the phase condition becomes a curve, and a comparison
argument gives a bound at every frequency, attained by one system with a
single zero. At a right angle that system is algebraic, and the bound
reads $\abs{H(\sqrt{ab})}^2\le a/b$.

The ingredients are classical: the Herglotz representation, Kramers--Kronig
relations, a Cauchy integral, an elliptic integral reduced by Landen's
transformation, Hardy spaces and a conformal map with a free parameter. What
is new, as far as we can tell, is the pair of sharp bounds, the
identification of the price of a constant phase with a single number
$\Lambda$ for zero-free systems, and the pointwise optimality of a single
system, with zeros allowed and, for an affine phase, without them.

\subsection{Open problems}

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] \emph{Delay together with zeros.} \Cref{thm:allzeros} solves
\cref{prob:zeros}, and \cref{cor:allweights}(ii) extends it to a phase
$\theta-\tau\omega$ with $\tau\ge0$. For $\arg H\equiv\theta+\tau\omega$ with
$\tau>0$ the system $e^{i\tau z}\Hs_\theta$ still has the loss $\as_\theta$; is it
optimal, or can a delay combined with zeros do better? The change of
variables of \cref{lem:transform} needs a constant phase.
\item[(2)] \emph{General phase without zeros.} \Cref{thm:reconstruction}
holds for every continuously differentiable phase $g$, and
\cref{cor:compare} is sharp whenever $h_g+\max\{0,-\beta_g\}R/z$ is a
Herglotz function. When $v_g$ changes sign the cheapest measure off the
band is not known; for a lead with positive slope, $-s+\tau\omega$ with
$\tau>0$, this is already the case.
\item[(3)] \emph{Several channels.} For a matrix Herglotz function $F$ with
the symmetry of \eqref{eq:Hh}, \cref{thm:reconstruction} applied to every
form $u^*Fu$ bounds $\Im F$ on the band from below in the Loewner order. The
least compensation for a matrix phase $\Theta+\omega T$ whose coefficients
do not commute is not known.
\item[(4)] \emph{Several bands.} For a phase that is constant on each of
several disjoint bands, the test function is built from a hyperelliptic
square root, and the constant becomes a period of a hyperelliptic
differential. We expect the sum rule to carry over with the same proof.
\item[(5)] \emph{Reflection and matching.} For reflection coefficients,
the Fano bound \cite{fano} and the present bound constrain different parts
of the same function. A joint bound on the modulus and the argument of a
reflection coefficient on a band would combine the two.
\item[(6)] \emph{The optimal system in closed form.} For $\theta\ne\pi/2$,
$\Hs_\theta$ comes from a conformal map onto the slit domain $D_T$ of
\cref{prop:exist}. The Joukowski map $G\mapsto\frac12(G+1/G)$ straightens the
semicircle of its boundary but bends the slit unless $\cos\theta=0$, which is
why the right angle is elementary. Is there a closed form for other
$\theta$, for instance through the Schwarz equation of the circular
quadrilateral?
\item[(7)] \emph{Formalisation.} The finite arithmetic of the paper is
checked in Lean (\cref{app:verification}). The analytic part, the sum rule
and the evaluation of $\Lambda$, would require a formalisation of boundary
values of Cauchy integrals, of the M.~Riesz theorem and of the complete
elliptic integrals with Landen's transformation, and the results on zeros
would need Hardy spaces, the M\"obius change of variables, extremal length
and the Carath\'eodory extension of conformal maps. We have not done this,
and the analytic theorems of the paper are not machine-checked.
\end{enumerate}
