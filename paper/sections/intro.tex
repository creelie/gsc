\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Does it cost energy to shift the phase of a wave? At one frequency it does
not. A delay line of length $\tau$ multiplies a wave of angular frequency
$\omega$ by $e^{i\omega\tau}$, and a suitable $\tau$ gives any phase without
loss.\footnote{The factor $e^{i\omega\tau}$ has period $2\pi/\omega$ in $\tau$, so a delay of at most one period gives every phase; at $5$\,MHz one degree takes $555.6$\,ps (\cref{sec:audit}).}
The question has content when the same shift is asked of every frequency in
a band. A delay cannot do that, since its phase grows with frequency. A
system that does it moves the oscillations of a pulse under a fixed envelope
(\cref{fig:pulses}).

The question was raised in an earlier version of this work by two of us
\cite{tb23,tb23pre}, where a $1^\circ$ shift of a 5\,MHz wave was
given a delay of $555$\,ps and an energy loss $\Delta E=hf\Delta t$. The delay
is right and the formula is not (\cref{sec:audit}): it has the units of an
action, and a fixed phase shift changes neither the frequency nor the energy
of a wave. The idea behind it, that a phase shift over a band has an energy
price, is correct, and this paper finds the price exactly.

\subsection{Main results}

A passive system is a transfer function $H$, analytic and bounded by $1$ in
the upper half-plane $\Cp$, with real impulse response. A pure delay is
$e^{i\omega\tau}$, the phase $\varphi(\omega)$ of $H$ is a lag when it is
positive, and $\alpha(\omega)=-\log\abs{H(\omega)}\ge0$ is the attenuation in
nepers.\footnote{One neper of amplitude is $20/\ln10\approx8.686$\,dB, and the power transmission is $\abs{H}^2=e^{-2\alpha}$.}
We call $H$ \emph{zero-free} when it has no zeros in $\Cp$; minimum-phase
systems and Foster's lossless resonances \cite{foster} are of this kind.

\begin{quote}
\emph{Let $H$ be zero-free and passive, continuous and nonzero near the band
$[a,b]$, $0<a<b$, with phase $\varphi(\omega)=\theta+\tau\omega$ on the band,
$\theta\ge0$, $\tau\in\R$. Then}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main}
\frac1{b-a}\int_a^b\alpha(\omega)\dd\omega\;\ge\;\theta\,\Lambda,
\qquad
\Lambda=\frac{K(\kappa)-E(\kappa)}{\kappa},
\end{equation}
\emph{where $\kappa=(b-a)/(b+a)$ and $K,E$ are the complete elliptic
integrals. An explicit system $H_\star$ attains the bound
(\cref{thm:main,thm:extremal}).}
\end{quote}

The delay $\tau$ does not enter: a system may delay the envelope freely, and
pays only for the offset $\theta$ between carrier and envelope. The constant
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-bounds}
\begin{gathered}
\frac\pi4\,\kappa\;<\;\Lambda\;\le\;\frac\pi4\,\frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa^2},\\
\Lambda\sim\tfrac12\log\frac{4b}{a}-1\quad(a/b\to0),
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
so a shift of one degree costs at least $0.0415$\,dB on an octave and
$0.143$\,dB on a decade (\cref{tab:lambda}), and on a narrow band about
$0.0137\,\Delta f/f$ of the power (\cref{cor:worst}).

The bound holds frequency by frequency. Every zero-free system is
$e^{i\tau z}H_\star$ plus terms, one for each part of its spectrum off the
band, and each raises the attenuation at every point of the band
(\cref{thm:reconstruction}). So one explicit system has the least
attenuation at every frequency at once, for a lag and for a lead with
$\tau\le0$ (\cref{thm:affine,thm:leadpoint}). A lossless resonance outside the band only
adds loss (\cref{rem:resonances}).

Zeros lower the loss, and we find by how much. For $0<\theta<\pi$ there is a
passive system $\Hs_\theta$ with one zero, on the imaginary axis, such that
every passive system with $\arg H\equiv\theta$ on the band has
$\alpha\ge\as_\theta$ at every frequency of the band (\cref{thm:allzeros}). For
a right angle it is algebraic,
\begin{gather*}
\Hs_{\pi/2}(z)=-\frac{z^2+ab}{R(z)+i(a+b)z},\\
R(z)^2=(z^2-a^2)(z^2-b^2),
\end{gather*}
its mean attenuation is $\frac\pi2(\sqrt b-\sqrt a)/(\sqrt b+\sqrt a)$, and no
passive system with this phase passes more than the fraction $a/b$ of the
power at $\omega=\sqrt{ab}$: on an octave the loss there is at least
$3.01$\,dB.

The last part of the paper adds a delay to the phase. Without zeros the least
mean loss for the phase $-s+\tau\omega$ is found in closed form for every
slope $\tau$ (\cref{thm:exactmean}), and it never falls below a fixed positive
floor, however long the delay (\cref{prop:floor}). With zeros allowed, the
optimal system exists and is unique for every phase, there is a simple
test for it (\cref{thm:convex}), and it is the limit of explicit passive
polynomials, each the solution of a finite convex problem
(\cref{thm:construction}). Its loss falls exponentially with the delay: the
exponent lies between $-\pi ab/(b-a)$ and
$-\frac{a+b}\pi\log\cot\frac{\pi(b-a)}{4(a+b)}$, which is $-1.2576$ on an
octave (\cref{thm:sharperate,cor:rate}); a device with six taps and the
delay $5$ loses less than $7\times10^{-4}$ nepers on an octave, less than an eighth of
what any device without zeros must lose (\cref{prop:sixtap}).
On several bands the price is again exact. For a union $B$ of $m$ bands and
the phase $\theta+\tau\omega$ on $B$ the mean loss without zeros is at least
$\theta\Lambda_B$, with an explicit constant $\Lambda_B$, and a system with
one lossless resonance in each gap attains it
(\cref{thm:severalbands}).\footnote{The resonances sit where the test
function of the bands vanishes; for two bands $[a_1,b_1]\cup[a_2,b_2]$ the
one resonance is at $c_1^2=(b_1^2b_2^2-a_1^2a_2^2)/(b_1^2+b_2^2-a_1^2-a_2^2)$.}
We also treat several channels (\cref{thm:trace,thm:noloewner}).

\subsection{Method}

The proof rests on one identity. If $H=\pm e^{ih}$ with $h$ a Herglotz
function, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-sum}
\int_a^b\alpha\dd\omega
=\frac12\int_a^b\varphi(\omega)\,p(\omega)\dd\omega
+\frac\pi4\int_{\R\setminus S}W\dd\mu ,
\end{equation}
where $S=[-b,-a]\cup[a,b]$, $\mu$ is the Herglotz measure of $h$,
\begin{gather*}
p(\omega)=\frac{a^2+b^2-2\omega^2}{\sqrt{(\omega^2-a^2)(b^2-\omega^2)}},\\
W=\frac{(\rho-1)^2}{\rho},\qquad
\rho(z)=\sqrt{\frac{z^2-a^2}{z^2-b^2}} ,
\end{gather*}
and $W$ is positive off the band (\cref{fig:testfunction}). It follows from
the Kramers--Kronig relations \cite{kronig,kramers,toll}. Since
$\int_a^b\omega p\dd\omega=0$ a delay drops out, and since $\int_a^bp\dd\omega>0$
a positive constant phase forces loss. With zeros we pass to $\zeta=\log F$,
$F=e^{-i\theta}(H-e^{i\theta})/(H-e^{-i\theta})$, where the zeros disappear and
the phase condition puts $\zeta$ on a fixed curve over the band; a comparison
argument in Hardy spaces then gives the pointwise bound. With a delay, the
problem with zeros becomes convex once it is written in terms of the
boundary values, and the exponential decay comes from a smoothed sign
function of exponential type.

Bode's gain--phase relations \cite{bode}, Fano's matching bound \cite{fano},
Rozanov's absorber bound \cite{rozanov}, the Herglotz bounds of Gustafsson,
Sj\"oberg, Bernland and Luger \cite{gustafsson,bernland} and Miller's
delay--bandwidth bound \cite{miller} are of the same family. None of them
bounds the loss forced by a frequency-independent phase shift, and neither
does the work on broadband phase shifters, where constant phase
\emph{differences} between lossless channels are built \cite{darlington}.
Other work of the authors, on gravity and on geometry, is in
\cite{bh22,bh22nc,bh22eq}.

\subsection{Plan}

\Cref{sec:audit} checks the equations of \cite{tb23}, and
\cref{sec:kinematics} separates a constant phase factor, a delay and a phase
that changes in time. \Cref{sec:passive,sec:test} set up passive systems and
the test function, \cref{sec:sumrule,sec:constant} prove the main theorem and
evaluate $\Lambda$, \cref{sec:extremal,sec:pointwise} give the extremal and
pointwise results without zeros, and \cref{sec:zeros,sec:allzeros} treat
zeros. \Cref{sec:beyond} adds a delay, several channels and several bands,
\cref{sec:waves} applies the results to waves, and \cref{sec:discussion}
lists what is open. \Cref{fig:roadmap} shows how the results depend on each
other. The appendices derive the facts on elliptic integrals, the integrals
of the weight and the extremal profiles, and describe the machine checks:
the finite and algebraic steps are certified in Lean~4, and a script
recomputes every number. Every statement is proved in the text.
